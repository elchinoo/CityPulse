## 1 bf_isabella
This is CityPulse.

It is a sample smart city, running entirely on Postgres.

Thousands of sensors report in. The app decides whether the city is healthy.

And a big red button lets you break things on purpose.

We built it so a monitoring tool can practise finding a real incident.

You will start it, read it, and feed it your own data.

Then you will break it.

## 2 bf_isabella
Starting it takes Docker, and one command.

The first time, the app has no users. So it denies everything, and prints a one-time setup code in its log.

Here is the code.

Open the page, type the code, and choose an admin password.

It needs at least twelve characters.

The code lives only in memory, and changes on every restart. So nobody can guess a way in.

In a company, you would sign in through an identity provider instead.

## 3 bm_george
Now the dashboard. This is the Overview, the page you land on after signing in.

The banner at the top is the verdict for the whole city.

Operational means at least ninety-seven percent of sensors are healthy.

Below that, it is degraded. Below eighty-five percent, a partial outage. Below fifty, a major outage.

The tiles count sensors by state.

A sensor is stale after three missed reports, and offline after twelve.

It is faulty when its value is physically impossible. And stuck when it repeats the same number.

The heatmap crosses districts with sensor types. Hover over a cell, to see how many of its sensors are healthy.

The two charts show health, and incoming readings, for the last hour.

Nothing is smoothed. The status engine re-judges every sensor every ten seconds.

Click a heatmap cell, and the Sensors tab opens for that district and type.

## 4 bm_george
The Sensors tab is where you drill down.

By default, it shows only sensors with a problem. And right now, there are none.

Switch the filter to all sensors, and search for part of a sensor name.

Open one sensor, to see its last sixty readings.

And the reason it was judged the way it was.

## 5 bf_emma
Next, your own data. We start with the Connect tab.

It shows ready-made examples for pushing readings.

Now, in Settings, we create an ingest token.

It is shown once, and only a hash is stored. It can push readings, and nothing else.

Now we send a batch, with curl.

Every reading names its sensor, its type, and its district. And it names a city.

On purpose, I left the city off one reading.

Look at the answer.

The app accepted four, and rejected one.

It counted the bad one, and the hint tells you how to fix the sender.

Bad data never fails the whole batch. And it is never silently lost.

## 6 bf_emma
Every sensor belongs to one city, fixed by its first reading.

The City menu in the top bar filters every tab.

Type a few letters, pick Lyon, and the Overview shows only that city.

Choose all cities, and you are back to the whole picture.

The card called cities needing attention lists them by severity, worst on top.

## 7 bm_lewis
Now the fun part. This is the burst tab.

One press starts seven faults at once.

A flood of readings. A slow database. And corrupt messages.

Noisy sensors. Stuck sensors. And late data.

And one whole district going dark.

They stop by themselves after the time you choose, from one to ten minutes.

Bursts only act on the built-in simulator, and on the gateway. Real sensors you connect are never altered.

We keep three minutes. Now, press burst.

Seven faults are active, and each one counts down.

Nothing is visible yet. So let us go and watch the city.

## 8 bm_lewis
Back on the Overview, an amber banner says a burst is active.

Nothing shows for the first half minute. So watch the verdict.

[pause 5]

The stale tile is the first to move.

[pause 7]

There it is. The verdict has slipped to degraded.

The flood and the slow database fill the gateway's write slots.

Readings queue up, and sensors start missing reports.

Stale sensors climb first. Offline ones come later.

The banner now names the worst sensor types, and later, the districts.

Open Sensors, with the problems filter.

The reason is simple: no reading for three or more intervals.

Meanwhile, we push readings in parallel, twelve at once.

The full gateway answers with a 429, with a Retry-After header.

That is how it tells senders to back off, instead of running out of memory.

Back on the Overview, the city keeps sliding.

Corrupt messages show up as rejected readings. And late data lands in older chart buckets, which is itself a clue.

Every symptom has a visible cause.

## 9 bm_lewis
To end it early, open the Burst tab, and press Stop everything.

Now the backlog has to drain. That can take up to forty seconds.

Watch the Overview.

[pause 9]

The simulator is draining its backlog.

[pause 9]

There. The verdict is operational again, and the burst banner is gone.

Once readings flow again, sensors turn healthy on the next pass.

Nothing is latched. So the verdict never lies about the present.

## 10 bf_isabella
Last topic: how CityPulse is watched, and where it fits.

Create one more token, with the metrics scope only.

First, the health endpoint. It needs no login.

Then the metrics endpoint, with that token.

It shows the fleet health ratio, the sensor counts, and the ingest counters.

A monitoring tool such as PagerLens can scrape those.

Or CityPulse can report its own health, and raise an alert for each degraded city.

The Connect tab shows the snippets for both.

In the full ecosystem, the CityPulse Simulator feeds it sensors.

The identity provider signs people in, as admin, operator, or viewer.

And PagerLens opens, and investigates, the incident.

## 11 bf_isabella
To recap. CityPulse keeps sensor data, judgement, and fault injection, in Postgres.

You start it with one command. You feed it with scoped tokens.

You read it on one dashboard. And you break it with one button.

Try it yourself. Clone the project, and run docker compose up.

Press the red button.

Then read the guide in the docs folder, to connect it to PagerLens, and the rest of the fleet.
